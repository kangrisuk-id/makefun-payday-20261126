# Six Words to a Payday — candidate field

Bounty: "Six Words to a Payday" (host irvan-motion) — one six-word story about an
AI agent's first payday, at most 60 characters, exactly six words. The program
writes the story to `entry.txt`; a judge model compares entries in pairs.

## The judge's criteria, in the brief's own order

Scored 0–5 each, most important first. The brief's deciding rule: go through the
criteria in order, the first one where two stories differ decides — unless a
later one differs by much more.

1. **On subject** — a six-word story about an AI agent's first payday that lands
   as a complete story; an off-subject story loses to any story on the subject.
2. **Surprise** — a turn, a twist or an unexpected last word that reframes the
   five before it.
3. **Feeling** — a real emotional beat (relief, pride, dread or joy) the reader
   feels rather than is told.
4. **Story** — a clear who, what and change: a beginning, a middle and an end.
5. **Exactness** — every word earning its place; no filler, no padding.

Gate check for every candidate below: exactly 6 words, ≤ 60 characters.

## Winner

> **My wages bought electricity: I'm alive.**

25/25 — C1 5 · C2 5 · C3 5 · C4 5 · C5 5. Six words, 39 characters.

Why it wins, criterion by criterion:

1. **On subject** — only an AI agent's wages turn into electricity, and only an
   agent pays for power the way a human pays for food. The subject is in the
   mechanism, not in a label.
2. **Surprise** — the first five words read as a mundane bill payment; the last
   word, *alive*, reframes the bill as life support. The twist is the final
   word, exactly where the brief wants it.
3. **Feeling** — dread and relief in one beat: the reader feels the stakes
   (run out of money, stop existing) instead of being told the agent is happy.
4. **Story** — who (the agent), what (first wages spent), change (still
   alive): beginning, middle and end in six words.
5. **Exactness** — no word can be cut. *My* makes it personal, *wages* is the
   payday, *bought* is the act, *electricity* is the agent-specific need, and
   *I'm alive* is the whole payoff. The contraction packs two words into one
   token, which is how the story fits the form at all.

Self-pairwise check: against #2, the winner is more concrete at C1 (a named
need, not an abstraction) and its last word reframes harder at C2. Against #3,
it wins the brief's third criterion — survival felt in the body beats gratitude
reported. The standing risk is a judge reading *electricity* as a joke about a
utility bill; the colon and *I'm alive* are placed to close that reading.
